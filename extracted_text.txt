ReliefPlan AI

1. Introduction
Natural disasters such as earthquakes, floods, cyclones, and armed conflicts often displace large numbers of people, requiring the rapid setup of temporary relief camps. Manual planning under such time pressure often leads to overcrowding, poor accessibility, and inefficient use of resources. ReliefPlan AI addresses this by automatically generating an optimized camp layout from satellite or drone imagery, combining terrain segmentation with a constraint-based layout optimization engine that positions tent blocks, roads, medical centers, water points, sanitation facilities, and emergency exits in line with established humanitarian planning standards such as those published by UNHCR.
2. Problem Statement
Emergency relief camps are currently designed manually by humanitarian planners working under severe time constraints, typically using GIS tools and printed planning handbooks. This manual process can result in inefficient use of available land, long walking distances to essential services, congestion in high-traffic areas, and inadequate emergency accessibility for vehicles and personnel.
This project proposes an AI-driven planning system capable of automatically generating optimized disaster relief camp layouts from satellite imagery using deep learning techniques, reducing planning time while improving safety and accessibility outcomes for displaced populations.
3. Motivation
ReliefPlan AI aims to assist disaster management agencies and humanitarian organizations by significantly reducing the time required to design emergency camps, while simultaneously improving accessibility, safety, and the allocation of critical resources such as water, sanitation, and medical care.
The project demonstrates how deep learning can move beyond traditional image classification tasks to automate humanitarian infrastructure planning, helping authorities respond faster and more effectively during emergencies, when every day of delay directly affects the health and safety of displaced people.

5. Literature Review / Related Academic Work
The Facility Layout Problem (FLP), which involves arranging a set of elements within a bounded area to optimize criteria such as travel distance and space utilization, is a well-studied combinatorial optimization problem in industrial and production engineering. Traditional approaches rely on systematic layout planning and mathematical optimization, which tend to be slow and difficult to adapt to new scenarios.
Recent research has applied Deep Reinforcement Learning to this problem. Studies using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), and Deep Q-Networks have shown that DRL agents can produce competitive facility arrangements and, in some cases, generalize across differently shaped layout problems without retraining, using image-based state representations. Open-source benchmarking environments such as gym-flp have been developed to standardize this line of research within the OpenAI Gym framework.
Separately, humanitarian organizations such as UNHCR have developed detailed settlement planning standards covering shelter spacing, sanitation ratios, and service coverage, based on decades of field experience. However, these standards are typically applied manually by planners rather than being encoded as machine-readable constraints. ReliefPlan AI bridges these two bodies of work: it treats humanitarian camp design as a layout optimization problem, similar in structure to industrial FLP research, while using UNHCR-style planning standards as the reward and penalty structure for a DRL agent operating directly on real satellite terrain data.

8. Proposed System Architecture
The system is organized into a five-stage pipeline:
Image Acquisition – accepts uploaded satellite or drone imagery of the disaster-affected site.
Terrain Segmentation – a convolutional segmentation network classifies each region of the image into categories such as open land, water body, vegetation, road, and existing structure.
Terrain Analysis – the segmented map is processed to identify the buildable area, compute its size, and flag unsuitable zones (water bodies, steep or unstable terrain, existing structures).
Constraint-based Layout Optimization Engine – an algorithm places tent blocks, roads, and essential facilities on the buildable area so as to satisfy humanitarian planning standards (see Section 9.2).
Blueprint Renderer – overlays the final generated layout on the original satellite image and exports it as a shareable camp blueprint.
Pipeline: Image Acquisition → Terrain Segmentation → Terrain Analysis → Constraint-based Layout Optimization Engine → Blueprint Renderer.

9. Methodology
9.1 Terrain Segmentation
Semantic segmentation of the input imagery will be performed using an encoder-decoder convolutional architecture such as U-Net or DeepLabV3+, both of which are widely used for satellite and aerial image segmentation. The output is a per-pixel classification map distinguishing buildable land from water bodies, dense vegetation, existing roads, and structures.
9.2 Camp Layout Optimization
This is the core of the proposed system. Once the buildable area has been identified, the optimization engine positions shelters, roads, and essential facilities so as to satisfy a set of humanitarian planning objectives simultaneously. The engine considers:
Buildable land only — tents and facilities are never placed on water bodies, unstable terrain, or existing structures
Shelter spacing — minimum distance maintained between shelters
Road connectivity — every shelter block must be reachable via the internal road network
Walking distance — shelters are placed to minimize distance to essential services
Water accessibility — shelters kept within standards-compliant distance of water points
Toilet accessibility — shelters kept within standards-compliant distance of sanitation facilities
Medical center accessibility — reasonable coverage of health facilities across the camp
Population capacity — the total number of people the buildable area can safely accommodate
9.3 Optimization Methodology
In the proposed implementation, the camp layout is generated using a constraint-based optimization algorithm that places shelters, roads, and essential facilities while satisfying the humanitarian planning standards listed above and in Section 9.4. This approach is directly implementable and testable within the scope of this course project.
In future work, this optimization engine can be replaced with a Deep Reinforcement Learning agent (e.g., trained with Proximal Policy Optimization) that learns optimal placement strategies through repeated simulation, rather than following a fixed optimization procedure. Section 14 elaborates on this extension.
9.4 Humanitarian Design Standards Encoded as Constraints
The optimization engine's scoring criteria are grounded in widely used UNHCR emergency settlement planning benchmarks, including:
Approximately 45 m² of total accessible camp area per person, of which around 30 m² is reserved for roads, footpaths, sanitation, firebreaks, and communal facilities.
A minimum of 3.5 m² of covered shelter area per person, with shelters spaced at least 2 m apart.
Approximately one shared latrine per 20 people and one shower per 50 people, located no more than 50 m from shelters.
Approximately one water tap stand per 80 people, located within 200 m of shelters.
Roughly one health center per 20,000 people and one food distribution point per 5,000 people.
These figures are used as configurable parameters in the simulation environment rather than fixed constants, so the model can be adapted to different guideline versions or local requirements.

Layout Optimization Criteria
The optimization engine does not require manually labeled camp layouts. Instead, it scores candidate layouts against the objectives and constraints described in Section 9.2 and Section 9.4. Example positive criteria include:
Short walking distance between tents and essential services
Good road connectivity throughout the camp
Proximity to medical facilities
Standards-compliant placement of toilets and sanitation points
Balanced camp density
Example constraint violations to avoid include:
Tent overlap
Construction on water bodies or unsafe terrain
Excessive travel distances to key facilities
Congestion in high-traffic zones
Unsafe or inaccessible layouts
As noted in Section 9.3, these same criteria are designed so that they can later serve as the reward and penalty structure for a Deep Reinforcement Learning agent, once that extension is undertaken.
11. Evaluation Metrics
The generated layouts will be evaluated using a combination of quantitative planning metrics and comparisons against manually designed layouts:
Average walking distance from shelters to water points, latrines, and medical facilities
Road connectivity index, measuring how well tent blocks are connected to the main road network
Standards-compliance rate, measuring adherence to spacing and ratio guidelines such as those in Section 9.4
Land utilization efficiency, comparing usable area to total buildable area
Planning time comparison between AI-generated layouts and estimated manual planning time for the same site

13. Novelty
Unlike existing disaster mapping systems that only analyze terrain, ReliefPlan AI automatically designs a complete disaster relief camp layout. Key innovations include:
Satellite image understanding using deep learning-based semantic segmentation
An AI-generated camp blueprint overlaid directly on the uploaded satellite image
A constraint-based layout optimization engine for autonomous camp layout generation, deliberately designed so it can later be replaced by a Deep Reinforcement Learning agent
Encoding real humanitarian planning standards (UNHCR-style spacing and service ratios) directly into the optimization engine's scoring criteria, rather than relying on generic optimization objectives
A focus on humanitarian infrastructure optimization rather than simple image classification

16. Conclusion
ReliefPlan AI demonstrates how deep learning can move beyond image classification to solve complex spatial planning problems. By combining satellite image segmentation with Deep Reinforcement Learning grounded in real humanitarian planning standards, the proposed system automatically generates optimized disaster relief camp layouts directly over satellite imagery.
